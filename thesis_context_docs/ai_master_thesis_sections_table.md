# Necessary Master Thesis Sections for an AI Project

> This table summarizes the recommended sections for a professional AI master thesis. The French **Résumé** section is intentionally removed. An English **Abstract** is kept because it is normally required in academic manuscripts.

| Order | Section | Main Question This Section Answers | Brief Description / What It Should Include |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 1 | Cover Page | What is the thesis, who prepared it, and under which institution? | University, faculty, department, specialty, thesis title, student name, supervisor, jury members, academic year, and degree name. |
| 2 | Acknowledgements | Who supported the work? | Short formal thanks to the supervisor, teachers, family, collaborators, laboratory, company, or institution that helped during the project. |
| 3 | Dedication *(optional)* | To whom is the work personally dedicated? | Personal dedication to family, friends, teachers, or people who supported the student. Keep it short and separate from the scientific content. |
| 4 | Abstract | What is the whole thesis about in one concise summary? | Briefly present the problem, motivation, proposed AI solution, dataset/method, implementation, main results, and contribution. Usually 150–300 words. |
| 5 | Keywords | What are the most important technical terms of the thesis? | 5–8 keywords such as Artificial Intelligence, Deep Learning, Computer Vision, YOLO, Edge AI, Regression, Dataset, Jetson Nano. |
| 6 | Table of Contents | Where can each part of the thesis be found? | Automatically generated list of chapters, sections, subsections, and page numbers. |
| 7 | List of Figures | Which figures are used and where are they located? | List of all diagrams, architectures, screenshots, charts, training curves, and result images with page numbers. |
| 8 | List of Tables | Which tables are used and where are they located? | List of all comparison tables, dataset tables, metric tables, hardware tables, and result tables with page numbers. |
| 9 | List of Abbreviations | What do the technical abbreviations mean? | Define abbreviations such as AI, ML, DL, CNN, API, GPU, IoU, mAP, RMSE, FPS, OCR, NLP, RAG, depending on the project. |
| 10 | Chapter 1: General Introduction | What problem does the thesis solve and why is it important? | Introduce the context, motivation, problem statement, research gap, objectives, scope, limitations, contributions, and thesis organization. |
| 11 | Chapter 2: Theoretical Background | What technical knowledge is needed to understand the project? | Explain only the necessary AI concepts: AI/ML/DL basics, model families, computer vision or NLP concepts, learning types, metrics, and domain-specific foundations. |
| 12 | Chapter 3: State of the Art and Literature Review | What already exists, what are its limitations, and where is the gap? | Review previous works, existing systems, datasets, algorithms, tools, and limitations. Compare approaches and end with the research gap your project addresses. |
| 13 | Chapter 4: Proposed Methodology / Proposed Approach | How does the proposed AI solution work? | Describe the full pipeline: data collection, annotation, preprocessing, model selection, architecture, training strategy, mathematical formulation, evaluation protocol, and baselines. |
| 14 | Chapter 5: System Design and Architecture | How is the complete system designed? | Present the software/system architecture, modules, UML diagrams if needed, database schema, APIs, workflows, actors, and deployment architecture. |
| 15 | Chapter 6: Implementation | How was the solution built in practice? | Describe tools, frameworks, programming languages, hardware, project structure, model training implementation, backend/frontend implementation, interfaces, and deployment steps. |
| 16 | Chapter 7: Experiments and Results | How well does the proposed solution perform? | Present experimental setup, dataset split, metrics, training results, test results, comparisons, graphs, confusion matrix, qualitative examples, and runtime performance. |
| 17 | Chapter 8: Discussion | What do the results mean and what are the limitations? | Interpret the results, explain model errors, compare with the literature, discuss limitations, threats to validity, practical usefulness, and possible improvements. |
| 18 | General Conclusion and Future Work | What was achieved and what should be improved later? | Summarize the problem, proposed solution, main achievements, results, limitations, and future directions. Do not introduce new technical content. |
| 19 | Bibliography / References | Which sources support the thesis? | Include all papers, datasets, official documentation, tools, frameworks, websites, and books used in the thesis using one consistent citation style. |
| 20 | Appendices *(optional)* | What extra material supports the thesis but is too detailed for the main text? | Add dataset details, configuration files, extra results, screenshots, API documentation, user guide, business/startup documents, or long code/config examples. |

## Recommended Minimal Structure

For a clean AI master thesis, the strongest minimal structure is:

1. Front matter: cover page, acknowledgements, abstract, keywords, contents, lists, abbreviations.
2. General introduction.
3. Theoretical background.
4. State of the art and literature review.
5. Proposed methodology.
6. System design and implementation.
7. Experiments and results.
8. Discussion.
9. General conclusion and future work.
10. Bibliography and appendices.

## Important Note for AI Theses

The thesis should not only describe the application. It must also prove the AI part scientifically. Therefore, the chapters about **methodology**, **experiments**, **results**, and **discussion** are essential. They show how the model was trained, how it was evaluated, what results were obtained, and what limitations remain.
